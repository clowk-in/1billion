Every 1billion workspace gets **100,000 credits every day**. A mail to one recipient costs 1,000 credits, so that is **100 mails a day, every day, for free**. No card, no trial that ends after 14 days, no plan that changes next year.

"Free forever" is a big promise. This post explains why we can keep it.

## We run our own mail servers

Some e-mail APIs are a layer over another provider, and they pay that provider for each mail. A free plan then costs them money on every send.

1billion has its own MTA: the servers that sign your mail with DKIM and talk SMTP to Gmail, Outlook and the rest. We pay for the servers, not for each mail. One more mail on a server that already runs costs us almost nothing. That is the whole reason a free plan is possible.

## A free workspace sends at a calm pace

The API answers `202` at once, and then each mail waits for its slot. A workspace on the credits of the day sends **1 mail every 3 seconds**. A burst of 100 login codes still goes out, a little slower; it never fails.

This pace is the rate limit that makes the offer safe. A free workspace can not turn into a firehose, and our servers never see a spike from it. 100 mails at 1 every 3 seconds is 5 minutes of work a day.

## The credits do not pile up

At midnight, in the time zone of your workspace, the balance goes back up to 100,000 credits. The credits of the day do not carry over. So the most that a free workspace can send is 100 mails a day, every day, and never 3,000 on the last day of the month.

## Bad mail is stopped before it costs anyone

Before the API accepts a mail, it checks each recipient, for free: the syntax, a domain that receives mail, no disposable inbox, no role address like `contact@`. A recipient that fails gets a `422`, and nothing is sent or charged.

An address that bounced goes to the suppression list, and we stop sending to it. Fewer bounces keep the reputation of the domain, and of our servers, high.

## The reputation of the servers is shared, so we guard it

All workspaces send from the same IP addresses. If one workspace sends spam, the mail of every other workspace can land in the spam folder. So when the reputation of a workspace falls under 90% in the last 30 days (with 100 sends or more), its sends pause, and a person looks at it.

![The E-mails page of the 1billion console: 37 mails sent, a 100% delivery rate, 0 bounces, and the workspace reputation at 100%, healthy.](/blog/free-forever/console.png)

*The console shows the reputation of each workspace and of each domain over 30 days, with the rule next to it: a warning under 95%, a pause under 90%.*

This is why the free plan is for transactional mail: login codes, receipts, resets, alerts. Mail that a person waits for, at a calm pace, is the best mail for a reputation.

## Who pays for it

The apps that grow past 100 mails a day buy a credit pack: **1 billion credits for R$ 59.99, paid once**. The credits never expire, there is no monthly fee, and the pace goes up to 5 mails a second. They pay for the servers, and the free plan stays free.

![The Credits page of the 1billion console: the credits left, the pack of 1 billion credits for R$ 59.99, and what cost credits in the last 30 days.](/blog/free-forever/credits.png)

*Each result has its price, and the console shows all of them: a mail the server took costs 1,000 credits, an address that does not exist costs 2,000, and a mail that we could not send, or that was blocked for our reputation, costs nothing.*

That is the deal: **you start free and stay free while you are small**. When your app sends more, you pay for what you send, once.
