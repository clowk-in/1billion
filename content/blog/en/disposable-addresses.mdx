A **disposable address** is a temporary inbox. A person opens a site of temporary e-mail, gets an address such as `x7k2@some-temp-mail.com`, uses it on your sign-up form, reads the code, and leaves. After some minutes or days, the inbox is gone.

There are thousands of these domains, and new ones come every day.

## Why people use them

- **To get past a wall.** Your product asks for an e-mail to show a trial, a download or a coupon. The person wants the thing, not your mail.
- **To get the free plan again.** A new address gives a new account, a new trial, or a new coupon.
- **Bots.** Sign-up bots and spam scripts use temporary inboxes because they cost nothing and leave no trail.

None of these people will read your next mail.

## Why they hurt your list

**They bounce later.** On the day of the sign-up, the address works. A week later, the inbox is gone, and your welcome series, receipts and news bounce. Bounces tell Gmail and Outlook that you do not clean your list, and that moves your mail toward spam, also for your real customers.

**They make your numbers wrong.** Each disposable address is a "user" who never comes back. Your sign-ups go up, but your opens, your activation and your conversion go down. You make decisions on data that is not true.

**They let people abuse your free plan.** One person with ten temporary inboxes is ten trials and ten coupons.

**They cost you money.** You pay to send each mail, also the mail that no one will ever read.

## How to stop them

The best moment is the sign-up form, while the person is still there. Ask for a real address, and say why: "Use an e-mail that you read. We send your codes and receipts there."

[`POST /validate`](/docs/api/validate) gives `disposable: true` for a temporary inbox, with the verdict `risky` and the reason `disposable` (score 0.2). It sends nothing:

```ts
const v = await mail.validate(email)

if (v.disposable) {
  return { error: "Use an e-mail that you read, not a temporary one." }
}
```

A validation costs 100 credits, one tenth of a mail.
