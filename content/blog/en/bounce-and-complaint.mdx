Two signals tell a mailbox provider that a sender is careless: bounces and complaints. They come from different places, but both make your next mail more likely to go to spam.

## A bounce: the server says no

A bounce is a refusal from the server of the recipient. It comes as an SMTP answer, with a code and a short text:

```txt
550 5.1.1 user unknown
```

The first digit tells the type:

- **`5xx`, a hard bounce:** a permanent failure. The mailbox does not exist, or the domain does not take mail. The next try gives the same answer.
- **`4xx`, a soft bounce:** a temporary failure. The mailbox is full, or the server is busy. A later try can pass.

A bounce can also come late: the server accepts the mail and then sends a bounce message to the envelope sender.

Many hard bounces tell the provider that you send to a list that you do not keep clean, for example a list that you bought or a list of old addresses.

## A complaint: the person says no

A complaint is a person who clicks **Report spam** on a mail that they got. The mail was delivered, but the recipient did not want it.

Some providers send the complaint back to the sender in a *feedback loop*. Gmail does not tell you which person complained, but it counts the rate, and its sender guidelines ask you to keep the spam rate under 0.3%.

A complaint usually comes from a person who:

- does not remember that they signed up,
- can not find how to stop the mail,
- gets more mail than they expected.

## How to have fewer of both

- **Check an address before you keep it.** A typo such as `gmial.com` becomes a hard bounce later.
- **Never send again to a hard bounce.**
- **Make it easy to leave.** A person who finds an unsubscribe link does not click "Report spam".
- **Send marketing from another domain.** Then a person who leaves your newsletter still gets your login mail.

## What 1billion does

**Before the send.** `POST /send` checks each recipient for free: the syntax, a domain that receives mail, no disposable inbox and no role address (`contact@`, `noreply@`). A recipient that fails gets `422`, and nothing is sent or charged. You can also call [`POST /validate`](/docs/api/validate) on a sign-up form, and send nothing.

**After a bounce.** The message gets the status `bounced`, and `last_error` has the answer of the server. Your app gets a `bounced` [webhook](/docs/guides/webhooks) with the same text in `detail`. A hard bounce puts the address on the [suppression list](/docs/api/suppressions) of the app: a new mail to it becomes a free message with the status `suppressed`. When the address works again, take it off with `DELETE /suppressions/{address}` or in the console.

**For complaints.** Turn on **List-Unsubscribe** on the domain. Each mail then has the one-click unsubscribe headers (RFC 8058), so Gmail and others show an *Unsubscribe* button next to the sender. A person who leaves gives an `unsubscribed` webhook, and stops getting mail from that domain only.

**The reputation of the workspace.** When the reputation of the last 30 days is under 90%, with 100 sends or more, the sends of the workspace are paused with `403`, and a person reviews it. This keeps one careless sender from hurting the others.
